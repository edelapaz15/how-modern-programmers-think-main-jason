# Activity 05: Tech Health Plan

**Module:** II (Productivity, Focus, and Well-Being)
**Related reading:** [Staying Healthy in Tech](../docs/Module-02-Productivity-Focus-and-Well-Being/03-staying-healthy-in-tech.md)

---

## Objective

By the end of this activity, you will have assessed your current habits, identified specific health risks from intensive screen work, and created a personalized one-page Tech Health Plan that you'll commit to for the duration of your course.

---

## Background

Here's a hard truth: intensive screen work takes a toll. Burnout is real. Repetitive strain injuries (RSI) are real. Sleep disruption is real. Eye strain is real. And career-changing adults often pressure themselves harder than they should because they feel like they're "behind" or have "something to prove."

This activity isn't about shame. It's about *prevention*. The best time to build healthy habits is now, at the beginning, before you develop problems. Think of this like a physical: you're checking in with yourself before problems happen.

The programmers who last—who have long, sustainable careers—are the ones who protect their health intentionally. They're not ascetics. They're pragmatists. A 30-minute walk clears your head better than an extra hour of frustrated coding. Sleep matters more than grinding. Your eyes are irreplaceable.

This activity asks you to get specific about your own risk factors and what will realistically work for you. It's a commitment to yourself.

---

## Instructions

### Part 1: Current Habits Audit (10 minutes)

Answer these questions honestly. This is for you only. Accuracy matters more than impressiveness.

**Sleep:**
- How many hours do you typically sleep per night?
- What time do you usually go to bed? Wake up?
- Do you use screens (phone, laptop) in the hour before bed?
- How rested do you usually feel?

**Movement & Exercise:**
- How much intentional exercise do you do per week? (Walking counts.)
- How many hours per day do you spend sitting?
- Do you have any neck, shoulder, or back pain?
- When was the last time you moved intentionally (walked, stretched, exercised)?

**Screen Time & Breaks:**
- On a typical day, how many hours do you spend looking at screens?
- How often do you take breaks while working on your computer?
- Do you have eye strain, headaches, or dry eyes?
- Do you use the 20-20-20 rule? (Every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds.) Do you even know what that is?

**Stress & Mental Health:**
- How stressed do you feel about your career transition right now? (Scale 1–10)
- Do you feel pressure to learn faster or be "further along"?
- How's your overall mood? (Anxious? Hopeful? Overwhelmed? Grounded?)
- Do you have a support system (friends, family, community)?

**Nutrition & Hydration:**
- Do you eat regular meals, or do you skip meals when focused?
- How much water do you drink per day?
- How much coffee/caffeine? (Be honest about time of day.)
- Do you have any dietary sensitivities or habits that affect your energy?

**Work Boundaries:**
- What time do you stop working each day?
- Do you work on weekends?
- Do you think about work when you're not working?
- How often do you completely disconnect from screens?

**Write it down.** It doesn't have to be perfect. Just honest.

### Part 2: Risk Assessment (5 minutes)

Based on your audit, identify your personal risk areas. These are the habits that, if continued for months or years, could cause problems.

**Common risk zones for career changers:**

- **Sleep deprivation** – Staying up late to learn, anxiety about progress, pressure to "catch up"
- **Sedentary lifestyle** – Spending 8+ hours sitting, no breaks, worsening posture
- **Eye strain** – No breaks from screens, poor lighting, screen positioned incorrectly
- **RSI (Repetitive Strain Injury)** – Wrist pain, shoulder pain, tendinitis from typing
- **Burnout** – Pushing too hard, no recovery time, perfectionism, fear of "falling behind"
- **Social isolation** – Intense focus on learning, withdrawing from non-coding relationships
- **Caffeine dependency** – Using energy drinks or coffee to compensate for poor sleep
- **Mental health** – Anxiety, imposter syndrome, isolation, comparing yourself to others

**Write down your top 3 risk zones.** For each, note: "This matters to me because..."

*Example: "Sleep deprivation is a risk for me because I'm trying to learn fast, and I tend to stay up late. But when I'm sleep-deprived, I code slower, make more mistakes, and feel anxious."*

### Part 3: Build Your Tech Health Plan (12 minutes)

Create a one-page document called `tech-health-plan.md`. It should be realistic, specific, and something you'll actually do—not something you *think* you should do.

**Your plan should include:**

#### Physical Health

**Eyes:**
- How often will you take breaks from screens? (e.g., every 30 minutes, 5-minute break)
- How will you remember? (Phone alarm? Timer app? Habit attached to something else?)
- What will you do during breaks? (Look out a window, close eyes, stretch neck)

**Movement:**
- How much intentional movement will you do per week? (Be specific: "30-minute walk 3x per week" not "exercise more")
- When will you do it? (Time of day matters. When are you most likely to actually do it?)
- What will you do? (Walking, yoga, dancing, sports—pick something you actually enjoy or will tolerate)

**Posture & Pain:**
- If you have pain (neck, shoulders, wrists), what will you do?
- Will you see a professional? Do stretches? Adjust your workstation?
- How often?

#### Sleep & Recovery

- What time will you stop working each day? (Set a hard boundary. Write it down.)
- What time will you aim to go to bed?
- What will you do to wind down 30 minutes before bed? (No screens is ideal. What's realistic for you?)
- One night per week, you'll completely disconnect from screens after [time]. When?

#### Nutrition & Hydration

- How much water will you aim for per day? (Aim: 8 glasses. Be realistic about your goal.)
- What will trigger you to drink water? (Every time you take a code break? A timer?)
- Will you eat regular meals, or are you a snacker? Whatever your style, be intentional.
- How will you prevent skipping meals when you're focused? (Set a lunch alarm? Pack snacks?)

#### Mental Health & Boundaries

- One thing you'll do to connect with others (not about programming): [frequency and activity]
- One thing that helps you feel grounded or calm: [frequency]
- If you feel overwhelmed, what will you do? (Talk to someone? Take a day off? Go for a walk?)
- How often will you revisit this plan? (Weekly? Monthly? Be realistic.)

#### Caffeine (if applicable)

- How much caffeine will you have per day?
- After what time will you stop consuming caffeine? (Too late in the day disrupts sleep.)

---

## Expected Deliverable

A one-page document (`tech-health-plan.md`) that includes:

1. **Your current baseline** (sleep hours, exercise per week, screen break frequency, stress level)

2. **Your top 3 risk zones** and why they matter to you

3. **Your specific, realistic commitments** in these areas:
   - Eye health and screen breaks
   - Movement and exercise
   - Sleep and bedtime boundary
   - Nutrition and hydration
   - Mental health and connection
   - Caffeine/stimulant use
   - One weekly disconnect practice

4. **Accountability** – How will you remember to follow this? Who will you tell? What will you check weekly?

**Example format:**

```
# My Tech Health Plan

## Current Baseline
- Sleep: 6 hours per night (want 7–8)
- Exercise: Walking 1x per week (not enough)
- Screen breaks: Almost never
- Stress level: 8/10 (worried about keeping up)

## My Top Risk Zones
1. Sleep deprivation – I stay up late learning and feel anxious
2. No movement – I sit 8+ hours and my back hurts
3. Burnout risk – I feel pressure to learn fast

## My Commitments

### Eye Health
- 5-minute break every 30 minutes
- Set phone alarm to remind me
- During breaks: look out the window, close eyes

### Movement
- 30-minute walk every weekday morning (before I start work)
- Yoga video 2x per week on weekends
- Stand up and stretch every 60 minutes (set timer)

### Sleep
- Stop working at 8 PM
- Bed by 10:30 PM
- No screens after 10 PM (read instead)
- Disconnect completely one evening per week (Sunday)

### Nutrition
- Eat breakfast (currently skipping)
- Lunch by noon (set calendar reminder)
- Water bottle at desk, aim for 8 glasses per day

### Mental Health
- Text a friend 2x per week (not about coding)
- Sunday morning walk with a friend
- If I feel overwhelmed, I'll take a day off instead of pushing harder

### Caffeine
- Max 2 cups of coffee per day
- No coffee after 1 PM

## How I'll Remember
- Phone alarms for breaks and lunch
- Weekly check-in: Sunday evening, review the plan
- Tell my friend [name] so they can ask how I'm doing
- Review this plan on [date]
```

## Reflection Questions

1. **What's one thing on your plan that you're genuinely worried you won't stick to?** What would make it more doable? (Maybe it's too ambitious, or the timing is wrong.)

2. **Which of your risk zones feels most serious?** What could happen if you don't address it over the next 3 months?

3. **Who will hold you accountable?** Is there someone you trust to ask you about this plan weekly or monthly?

4. **What's one small thing on your plan that you're confident you *will* do?** Start there. Small wins build momentum.

---

## Tips for Success

- **Be realistic, not inspirational.** "I'll exercise 6 days a week" sounds impressive. "I'll walk 20 minutes on weekday mornings" is something you might actually do. Pick the latter.
- **Attach new habits to existing ones.** If you always have morning coffee, do your stretches while the coffee brews. If you eat lunch at noon, set a water-bottle reminder at 11:45.
- **Start with one or two things.** If you try to change everything at once, you'll fail at all of it. Pick the two most important changes and nail those first.
- **Expect to miss days.** You'll skip a walk. You'll stay up late. You'll forget the water. That's not failure. It's life. Get back on track the next day.
- **Your plan should feel sustainable.** If it feels like punishment, you won't stick to it. This is an investment in yourself.
- **Check in regularly.** Weekly is ideal. Monthly is fine. Every three months is too long—you'll have forgotten the plan.

---

## Commitment Statement

At the end of your plan document, write this:

> "I commit to this Tech Health Plan because my well-being is as important as my learning. I understand that burnout and injury would actually slow my progress. By protecting my health, I'm protecting my ability to learn and build a sustainable career."

Sign it (or type your name). Make it real.

---

## Optional: Share Your Plan

If you're in a cohort or group, consider sharing your plan with a peer. Ask them to check in with you monthly. Real accountability—not judgment, but genuine check-ins—is powerful.
