# Activity 03: Design Your Developer Workspace

**Module:** II (Productivity, Focus, and Well-Being)

## Objective

By the end of this activity, you will have audited your current physical and digital workspace, applied ergonomic principles to improve your setup, organized your development environment, and created a documented workspace plan that supports focus and well-being.

---

## Background

You're about to spend a lot of time at a desk, staring at a screen, and typing code. How that environment feels—literally *feels*—matters far more than you might think.

Programmers who skip this step often suffer from:

- **Neck and shoulder pain** from poorly positioned monitors
- **Wrist strain** from bad keyboard/mouse setup
- **Eye fatigue** from bad lighting or screen glare
- **Lower back pain** from sitting hunched over
- **Attention problems** from digital clutter and distractions
- **Decision fatigue** from searching for files and resources

The good news: this is fixable. Professional developers invest in their workspace. It's not indulgent; it's necessary. Your workspace is a tool. A dull saw doesn't get sharper by pushing harder—you sharpen the tool.

This activity walks you through auditing what you have, planning what you need, and organizing both your physical and digital environment. Small changes here have big returns in productivity and well-being.

## Instructions

### Part 1: Physical Workspace Audit (10 minutes)

Look at your current desk setup. Evaluate honestly:

**Monitor & Screen:**
- How far is your screen from your eyes? (Ideal: arm's length away, about 20–26 inches)
- Is the top of the monitor at or slightly below eye level when you sit naturally?
- Is the screen directly in front of you, or at an angle?
- Do you have glare from a window or light source?

**Chair & Sitting Position:**
- Can you sit with your feet flat on the floor and knees at 90 degrees?
- Is your back supported? (Good lumbar support is critical.)
- Are your elbows at roughly 90 degrees when you rest them on the desk?
- Do you find yourself slouching or leaning forward?

**Keyboard & Mouse:**
- Is your keyboard at the same height as your elbows?
- Is your mouse close to the keyboard, not stretched out?
- Do your wrists stay straight when typing, or do they bend up or down?

**Lighting:**
- Is your main light source above and behind your monitor (not in front)?
- Do you have glare on your screen?
- Is the room too dim (causing eye strain) or too bright?

**Other:**
- What's on your desk? (What's essential, and what's distraction?)
- Are your most-used items within arm's reach?
- Does your desk feel organized or chaotic?

**Write down:** For each area, note one thing that's working and one thing that could improve. Be specific. ("Monitor is too low" is better than "monitor position is bad.")

### Part 2: Physical Workspace Plan (10 minutes)

Based on your audit, make a practical improvement plan. You don't need to buy everything today. Just decide what matters most.

Create a simple list with three sections:

**This Week (Free or Very Cheap):**
- Adjust your monitor height (use a box or laptop stand)
- Move your desk to reduce glare
- Clear unnecessary items from your desk
- Adjust your chair or add a cushion
- Rearrange for better arm position

**This Month (Under $100):**
- Basic ergonomic keyboard or mouse
- Monitor stand or adjustable desk
- Desk lamp with adjustable brightness
- Footrest

**Future (As Budget Allows):**
- Ergonomic chair
- Dual monitors
- Standing desk

**Document your choices.** Write down: "I will do X this week because Y."

### Part 3: Digital Workspace Organization (15 minutes)

Now you'll organize your computer. This prevents decision fatigue and makes development faster.

**Step 1: Create a Development Folder Structure (5 minutes)**

On your computer, create this folder structure in your home directory or Documents:

```
dev/
├── projects/           (Where you'll store coding projects)
├── learning/           (Tutorials, notes, courses)
├── resources/          (Documentation, cheat sheets, reference materials)
├── tools/              (Configuration files, scripts you use often)
└── temp/               (Temporary files, throwaway experiments)
```

If you're on Windows, you might create: `C:\Users\YourName\dev\`
If you're on Mac/Linux, you might create: `~/dev/`

Create actual folders now. (Your instructor may guide you on how to do this if you're not familiar.)

**Step 2: Set Up Browser Bookmarks (5 minutes)**

Create a bookmark folder called "Dev Resources" and add bookmarks to:

- **Official documentation** for languages/tools you're learning (Python docs, JavaScript docs, etc.)
- **Learning resources** you keep returning to (docs, tutorials, references)
- **Learning platforms** you're using (if applicable)
- **Stack Overflow** (a critical resource for debugging)
- **GitHub** (where open-source code lives)
- **MDN Web Docs** (if you're learning web development)

Organize them into subfolders if it helps. The goal: when you need a resource, you know where to find it without searching.

**Step 3: Create a Daily Routine Template (5 minutes)**

Create a simple text file called `daily-routine.md` in your `dev/` folder with this structure:

```
# My Daily Development Routine

## Start of Day (First 10 minutes)
- [ ] Check which task I'm working on
- [ ] Open necessary tools (code editor, browser, etc.)
- [ ] Set my focus timer

## Deep Work Block (60-90 minutes)
- [ ] Work on one task
- [ ] No email or Slack
- [ ] Minimize browser tabs

## Break (5-10 minutes)
- [ ] Stand up
- [ ] Look away from the screen
- [ ] Get water/tea

## Mid-Day Review (5 minutes)
- [ ] What progress did I make?
- [ ] What's blocking me?
- [ ] What's next?

## End of Day (10 minutes)
- [ ] Document what I did
- [ ] Note what I'll do tomorrow
- [ ] Close all work applications
```

Fill in this template with your own timing and preferences.

### Part 4: Document Your Workspace Plan (5 minutes)

Create a file called `workspace-plan.md` in your `dev/` folder and include:

1. **Physical Setup**
   - Current state summary (2–3 sentences)
   - Improvements you'll make this week
   - Timeline for other improvements

2. **Digital Organization**
   - Your folder structure (you can describe it or paste the structure)
   - Where you'll store projects, learning materials, and resources
   - Where you've bookmarked key resources

3. **Daily Routine**
   - Your preferred working blocks and break schedule
   - Any environmental setup (music, no music, coffee, etc.)

4. **Review Schedule**
   - How often will you revisit this plan? (Weekly? Monthly?)

## Tips for Success

- **Start small.** You don't need a fancy setup. You need a *functional* setup. Many professional developers work in modest spaces.
- **Adjust over time.** Your ideal setup will evolve as you spend more time coding. Check in monthly.
- **Invest in your body first.** Screen position, chair, and desk height matter more than fancy peripherals.
- **Keep it real.** If you have kids or roommates, your workspace might not be pristine. That's okay. Functional beats perfect.
- **Lighting often fixes 80% of eye fatigue.** This is low-cost and high-impact. Don't skip it.

---

## Optional Extensions

- **Time yourself.** Spend a day without improvements, then a day with them. Do you notice a difference in focus or energy?
- **Research ergonomics.** Look up "proper monitor height" or "keyboard position for programming." You'll find specific measurements and reasons why they matter.
- **Connect with peers.** Ask other developers (in your course or online) about their setups. What works for them? You'll get creative ideas.
