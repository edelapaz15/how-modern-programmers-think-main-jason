# Activity 02: Decode a Software Team

**Module:** I (Introduction to Modern Programming)
**Related reading:** [Defining the Modern Programmer](../docs/Module-01-Introduction-to-Modern-Programming/01-defining-the-modern-programmer.md)

---

## Objective

By the end of this activity, you will understand the roles and responsibilities within a modern software team, recognize how different roles depend on one another, and map those roles to professional roles you've encountered in your previous career.

---

## Background

One of the biggest shifts in moving into programming is understanding that *programming is not a solo sport*. A modern software team is made up of many different roles, each with different expertise and responsibilities. Understanding these roles early helps you:

1. **Know what to expect** when you join a team
2. **Appreciate the work of non-developers** – and realize you'll depend on them
3. **See where you might naturally fit** based on your interests and strengths
4. **Communicate better** with teammates who have different perspectives

This activity gives you a realistic scenario and asks you to unpack it. You'll play detective: reading a scenario, identifying the roles in play, and connecting them to work you've seen before.

---

## The Scenario

**TechCart Inc.** is a 40-person software company building an e-commerce platform. Customers use the TechCart platform to run online stores. The company has been around for 3 years, has stable revenue, and is planning to launch a major new feature this quarter.

**The Initiative:** TechCart is building a real-time inventory tracking system. Customers will see live stock updates so they never oversell products. This feature touches almost every part of the system—the website customers see, the backend servers that handle orders, the database that stores inventory, and the mobile app. It's complex, and it touches the whole business.

**Here's what needs to happen:**

1. Someone needs to figure out **what** to build – What exactly will this feature do? What will it look like? How will it improve the customer experience?

2. Designers need to **design** how it looks and works – What buttons will users click? How will the interface work?

3. **Back-end developers** need to build the servers and logic that actually track inventory and update in real time.

4. **Front-end developers** need to build the website interface that displays the inventory to customers.

5. A **mobile developer** needs to update the mobile app to show live inventory too.

6. **QA engineers** (testers) need to check that the system works correctly. Does it handle 1,000 simultaneous customers? What if the internet cuts out? What if there's a bug?

7. Someone needs to think about **deployment** – How do we roll this out to customers without breaking anything? What if something goes wrong in production?

8. **Product Managers** and **Team Leads** need to coordinate all of this, manage timelines, and make sure everyone has what they need.

## Instructions

### Step 1: Identify the Roles (5 minutes)

In the TechCart scenario, identify at least 7 different roles. For each role, write down:

- **Role name** (e.g., "Product Manager")
- **What they do in this project** (one sentence)
- **What they care about** (What's their main concern or goal?)

Use this list as a reference, but try to derive it yourself first:

- **Product Manager / Product Owner** – Defines requirements and priorities
- **Designer / UX Designer** – Designs the user interface and experience
- **Front-End Developer** – Builds the website interface
- **Back-End Developer** – Builds the server-side logic and APIs
- **Mobile Developer** – Builds the mobile app
- **QA Engineer / Test Engineer** – Tests the software for bugs and edge cases
- **DevOps Engineer** – Manages deployment and infrastructure
- **Engineering Manager / Tech Lead** – Coordinates the team and unblocks issues

### Step 2: Day-in-the-Life for Three Roles (10 minutes)

Pick three of the roles from your list. For each role, write a short paragraph (3–4 sentences) describing what a typical day might look like for that person on the TechCart inventory project. Get specific and concrete.

*Example: "A QA Engineer on this project spends the morning writing test cases to cover different scenarios (what if 10,000 people try to buy the same item?). She runs those tests manually and documents bugs. By afternoon, she's working with back-end developers to understand why orders are processing in the wrong sequence. She's constantly thinking: 'How could this break?'"*

### Step 3: Map to Your Previous Career (10 minutes)

Think about roles you've worked with, supervised, or collaborated with in your previous career. These might be from healthcare, education, business, manufacturing, government, or anywhere else.

For each of the three roles you described above, identify a parallel role from your previous career. Write down:

- **The programming role**
- **The parallel role from your past**
- **Similarities** – What do they have in common? (Decision-making, quality control, coordination, focus on detail, etc.)

*Example: "QA Engineer is similar to a Nurse Auditor in healthcare. Both are checking for safety, compliance, and quality. Both document everything. Both are the people who catch what others miss. Both have to think like an adversary—'what could go wrong?'"*

### Step 4: Create a Role Comparison Chart (5 minutes)

Create a simple chart (on paper, in a spreadsheet, or a text table) with these columns:

| Programming Role | Day-to-Day Focus | How I've Seen This Role Before | Key Skill I Already Have |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product Manager | Define what to build, gather requirements, set priorities | Project Manager at [previous company] | I already understand how to gather stakeholder needs |
| Designer | Create the interface, think about user experience | [Your example] | [Your example] |
| QA Engineer | Find bugs, test edge cases, ensure quality | [Your example] | [Your example] |

## Expected Deliverable

A document containing:

1. **A list of 7+ roles** you identified in the TechCart scenario, with one-line descriptions of what each person does
2. **Three detailed day-in-the-life paragraphs** (one for each role you chose)
3. **Your role comparison chart** (similar to the example above) showing how programming roles parallel roles from your previous career

## Reflection Questions

1. **Which role surprised you most?** Was there a role on the team you hadn't thought about before? What did that person contribute that you initially overlooked?

2. **In your previous career, whose work did you depend on most?** Now that you understand software teams better, which programming role do you think that person is most similar to? Why?

3. **If you had to join a software team right now, which role—other than "developer"—do you think you'd most naturally gravitate toward, and why?** (This isn't a commitment; just reflective.)

---

## Tips for Success

- **Be concrete.** "They check things" is vague. "They write automated tests that run 10,000 times to catch bugs" is concrete.
- **Think systemically.** None of these roles exist in isolation. Each one depends on the others. Notice those dependencies.
- **Honor non-development roles.** It's easy for developers to be the "heroes," but the truth is that QA, DevOps, Product, and Design are equally critical. A team without them would fail.
- **Remember your own history.** You've worked in systems before. That experience is valuable. Use it.
