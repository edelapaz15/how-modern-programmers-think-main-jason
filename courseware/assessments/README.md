# Assessments — Formative Checks for Understanding

*Low-stakes, non-punitive checks that help the learner and the instructor see what stuck — never graded, never ranked.*

---

## Purpose

These are **formative** assessments, not exams. Their only job is to surface what landed and what is still fuzzy *while there is still time to do something about it*. They are designed for **adult learners** — government professionals being reskilled — so they are built on a few principles:

- **Non-punitive.** Nothing here is scored, graded, or compared between people. A blank or "I'm lost" answer is *useful data*, not a failure.
- **For the learner first.** Writing down what you can and can't recall is itself a proven way to strengthen memory and notice your own gaps. You keep your card.
- **For the instructor second.** Patterns across the room tell the instructor what to re-teach before the next layer is stacked on top.
- **Fast and light.** Each check fits in the last ~5 minutes of a module. No prep, no stress.

Adults learn best when they can see *why* something matters and where they stand. These checks make both visible without the anxiety of a test.

---

## What the two tools are

### Exit ticket
A short set of recap questions handed out (or shown) at the **end of each module**. Learners answer quickly from memory, hand it in (anonymously if they prefer), and move on. Each ticket here has:

- **3–4 quick recap questions** tied to that module (a mix of multiple-choice, short-answer, and one "explain in your own words"). The four-question tickets (Modules IV–VII) cover the modules with the most ground to check and run a couple of minutes longer.
- **One "Muddiest Point" prompt** — the single thing still unclear.
- **One "Connect it" prompt** — tie the module to the learner's own work experience or to the week's running example, **CivicTrack** (the City of Rivervale 311-style service-request system used across all five days).
- An **Instructor Answer Key** at the bottom for quick review.

### "Muddiest Point" card
A one-line technique borrowed from college teaching: *"What's the one thing from this module that's still fuzzy?"* It is built into every exit ticket as its own prompt. Even confident learners usually have one — naming it is the point. The instructor reads these first, because they map directly to what needs a second pass.

---

## How an instructor uses them

1. **At the end of each module** — hand out or display that module's exit ticket. Reassure the room: not graded, anonymous is fine, "I don't know" is a valid answer.
2. **Collect them** (paper, a shared doc, or a quick form — whatever fits the room).
3. **Review before building further.** Skim for patterns: Which recap question did several people miss? What "Muddiest Point" keeps coming up? That is your warm-up agenda for the next session.
4. **Reopen** by spending a few minutes addressing the top one or two gaps before stacking the next module on top. This closes the loop and shows learners their input changed the class.
5. **Never grade, rank, or single anyone out.** The moment these feel like a test, learners stop being honest and the signal disappears.

> **Tip:** The answer keys are concise on purpose. For the open-ended questions they say *what to listen for* rather than giving one "right" answer — there usually isn't just one.
