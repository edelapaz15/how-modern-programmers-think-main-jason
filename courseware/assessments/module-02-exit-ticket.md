## Quick Recap (3 questions)

**1. (Multiple choice)** We said programming is "an endurance sport for your mind." Which habit best supports *sustainable* long-term growth?

- A. Working in focused blocks with regular breaks, and protecting sleep and ergonomics
- B. Studying a little every single day, never taking a day off
- C. Pushing through whenever you're stuck, since giving up on a problem is a bad habit
- D. Putting in the longest hours you can while you're new, then easing off once it clicks

**2. (Short answer)** What is the **Pomodoro Technique**, in one sentence? (What does it actually ask you to do?)

**3. (Explain in your own words)** Programming rewards *attention to detail* — a single mistyped character can break a program. Describe one habit from this module for catching mistakes or keeping your focus during careful work.

<details>
<summary><strong>Instructor Answer Key</strong> (review before building on this module — collapsed for in-class use)</summary>

**1.** A — sustainable habits: focused work blocks with real breaks, plus sleep, movement, and an ergonomic setup. The distractors are all plausible-sounding discipline advice, which is the point. B sounds virtuous but has no rest in it. C is how people burn an afternoon on a bug that a walk would have solved in ten minutes — stepping away *is* a technique. D is the classic career-changer plan, and it's the one that produces week-three burnout.

**2.** A focused work interval (commonly ~25 minutes) followed by a short break, repeated — to protect attention and avoid burnout. Accept any answer capturing "timed focus block + break."

**3.** *Listen for:* deliberately slowing down when speed isn't helping, reading code carefully line by line, reducing distractions, single-tasking, taking breaks to reset attention, or re-checking work. Any one solid habit is enough.

**Muddiest Point / Connect It:** Not graded. This module's ideas are easy to nod along to and hard to practice — watch for "I know I should, but…" and normalize starting with one habit. Strong "Connect it" answers show learners transferring a proven personal system rather than starting from zero.
