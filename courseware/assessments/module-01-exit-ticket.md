## Quick Recap (3 questions)

**1. (Multiple choice)** We described the modern programmer as more than someone who memorizes code. Which of these best captures the idea?

- A. A problem-solver who knows how to find answers, collaborate, and learn continuously
- B. Someone who has memorized the syntax of at least one language thoroughly
- C. Someone who can write working code quickly without needing to look things up
- D. Someone whose main skill is producing a large volume of code

**2. (Multiple choice)** On a software team, which role is mainly responsible for deciding *what* gets built and in what order (the priorities), rather than writing the code?

- A. QA / Tester
- B. Product Manager
- C. Operations (Ops)
- D. Reviewer

**3. (Short answer)** Name **one** technology *layer* you'd find behind an everyday app (think of how we took an app apart — e.g., CivicTrack).

<details>
<summary><strong>Instructor Answer Key</strong> (review before building on this module — collapsed for in-class use)</summary>

**1.** A — the modern programmer is a continuous learner, collaborator, and problem-solver who knows *how to find things out*, not a memorizer. B and C are the beliefs most people walk in with, and they're worth naming out loud: professionals look things up constantly, and nobody holds a whole language in their head. D is the one to push back on hardest — more code is usually the wrong goal, and Module IV's "8% writing, 60% maintaining" figure is the reason why.

**2.** B — Product Manager (decides the *what* and the priorities). Contrast with developers who build it, QA who verifies it, ops who runs it.

**3.** Any reasonable layer: front end / UI, back end / logic, data store / database, API, plus surrounding tools and people. (CivicTrack mapping: web form = front end, routing logic = back end, requests/categories/departments tables = data store, the JSON endpoint = API.) Listen for the *concept* of layers, not perfect terminology.

**Muddiest Point / Connect It:** Not graded. Scan for recurring fuzzy topics to address in a quick warm-up. The "Connect it" answers are a good gauge of whether the team-roles framing landed; a strong match shows transfer from prior experience.
